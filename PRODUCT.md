# Product

## Register

product

(The landing page and the ladder band are brand surfaces inside the product; they use the brand register's permissions — committed colour, fluid display type, one orchestrated entrance.)

## Users
Clients (studio leads, indie developers, small brands) hiring freelancers for visual and written work, usually at a desk, comparing a few portfolios before deciding. Freelancers checking where they stand, uploading work, and answering authenticity matches.

## Product Purpose
FreeRank is a freelance marketplace with two arenas. Casual gives everyone whose work fits a request an equal turn. Competitive ranks freelancers by paid client work and a checked portfolio, and it's where clients who want proven quality go. Success means a client can see why someone ranks where they do, and nobody can buy, tag or steal their way up.

## Brand Personality
Earned, exact, athletic. The ranks work like sporting standings: numbers you can trust, insignia you're proud of. The voice is confident and plain, never salesy.

## Anti-references
Generic freelance marketplaces with oversized star ratings, badges everywhere and green CTAs. Esports neon, and black-and-gold "luxury". SaaS dashboard slop: glass, stat tiles, numbered eyebrows.

## Design Principles
- Rank is shown as evidence: every rank, score and "matched because" traces to a real number.
- Fairness is visible: Casual looks and behaves equal (no rank badges, a reshuffle you can press); Competitive is openly unequal.
- The work is the hero: portfolio images get the space, chrome stays out of the way.
- A match is a question, not a verdict: authenticity UI explains, offers an appeal, and never accuses.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG AA contrast; keyboard-operable everything; reduced motion respected (the only ambient motion is the emblem float and tilt). Thai and English input.
